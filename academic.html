Research interests

[RI-01] computer networking & distributed systems

I study computer networking and distributed systems. My interest is protocols, not the specification but the behaviour. What happens when implementations meet real conditions. Loss, delay, interference. My position is simple. A claim about network behaviour should be earned on the wire, not asserted. One dictum governs my MSc work: reproduce everything. Pinned commits. Declarative test plans. Automatic verdicts. The dissertation below applies the method end to end.

Open questions

  1. Reproducibility: can pinned commits, declarative test plans, and automatic verdicts become the standard way to evaluate Internet drafts?
  2. Conditions: how do QUIC, HTTP/3, and their extensions behave under loss, delay, and tight bandwidth or queue limits? What changes on mobile, satellite, or edge networks?
  3. Interop: where do independent implementations of QUIC extensions diverge on the wire? How early can tooling catch it?
  4. Benchmarking: what makes a fair, reproducible comparison between transport protocols?

These questions are a beginning, not a boundary. I am open to other problems in computer networking.

Dissertation

[TH-01] supervisor: prof. colin perkins

A Reproducible Testbed for Media over QUIC Transport: Interop, Controlled Impairments and Baselines. Supervised by Prof. Colin Perkins. The testbed runs on ContainerNet and Docker. It evaluates three MOQT implementations across basic, chain, and fan-out topologies, testing interoperability and delivery.

  • Draft support is checked on the wire with ALPN handshake tiers. Delivery is checked per subscriber with byte-hash verification.
  • Found an insidious fan-out bug in a production relay. It logged every subscriber as healthy, yet forwarded data to only one. I confirmed this was version-specific against an earlier pin.
  • Applied per-link loss, delay, bandwidth, and queue limits with Linux tc. Benchmarked MOQT against LL-DASH over HTTP/2 and HTTP/3. H3 gained ∼37% over H2. MOQT reached ∼1.95× same-content H3 delivery.
  • Everything reproduces. Pinned commits. YAML test plans. Automatic verdicts. Code: github.com/Hardik-7892/moqt-testbed.
